1The verdict

Valuation-sensitive

Orient Cables is a genuine specialist — twenty-one years old, certified to the levels its customers demand, backward-integrated into copper and PVC, holding roughly 22.9% of a real if narrow ₹3,590cr Indian networking-cable niche, and growing into telecom, data-centre and solar demand that is not imaginary. But at ₹272 an investor is not being asked to buy that business; they are being asked to buy one quarter. The June-2026 quarter produced 41.7% of the prior full year's revenue and 61% of its profit at an EBITDA margin three percentage points above the year just closed, and the auditor's own emphasis of matter confirms that no comparative period is presented anywhere in the 491 pages — so the number underpinning the marketed multiple of roughly 23x cannot be tested for seasonality, for year-on-year growth, or against the ordering pattern of the single unnamed customer who took 38.54% of it. Dig into the physicals and the quarter is +15% on volume and +46% on realisation, with the entire margin expansion coming from employee and other costs falling as a percentage of a spiking revenue line — operating leverage, which reverses. On the audited full year that can be tested, FY2026, the cap price is 51.6x the RHP's own EPS, 57.5x post-issue earnings and 62.8x after stripping ₹61mn of creditor write-backs and FX gains: more than Polycab at 46.7x and twice Finolex at 26.3x, both of them vastly larger, structurally more profitable and cash-generative. And FY2026 itself was a year of deterioration disguised by a 42% revenue line — PAT up 0.45%, EBITDA margin down from 10.17% to 8.23%, RoCE from 36.46% to 23.82%, net debt/EBITDA from 1.36x to 2.31x, and operating cash flow negative for a second consecutive year. Cumulatively across FY2025, FY2026 and the June quarter, this company generated −₹481.93mn of operating cash against ₹1,396.65mn of reported profit, with ₹727mn of receivables factored off balance sheet and ₹550mn of trade payables refinanced into borrowings quietly improving how both the working-capital cycle and the cash flow appear. The forensic work is not damning — related-party dealing is trivially small, there are no pledges, no discounted pre-IPO allotments, no material tax litigation, no CWIP parking, and the audit opinions are unmodified — but it is not comfortable either: CARO clause (ii)(b) recurs in all three years with a ₹348mn receivables discrepancy against the books at March 2026, the only external credit opinion ever issued is CRISIL B/Stable (Issuer not cooperating), an MCA notice landed five days before the RHP, and the company's own name is in litigation with Orient Electric. The IPO does real good — ₹1,555mn of debt repaid is worth roughly 17% of FY2026 earnings, and the promoters cut their OFS from ₹380cr to ₹232cr and keep 82.17% — but the fresh money repairs a balance sheet stretched by growth that was never self-funding, and it does not provide for the further ~₹1,500mn of working capital the June run rate implies for FY2027. The honest summary is a base case of roughly an 11% IRR over three years with a 30% probability of losing 60%, in a sector where the comparable that has risen 560% in twelve months is the reason the RHP can print a 128x industry average P/E. That is a fair business at a price that requires everything to go right. Pass at the band; revisit below ₹200, or after two quarters of post-listing numbers reveal whether June was a run rate or an event.

Would be attractive at: Below roughly ₹200 per share — about ₹2,280cr post-issue market cap, 26% below the ₹272 cap. That is ~20x a deliberately conservative normalised FY2027 PAT of ~₹110cr (midway between FY2026's audited ₹53.6cr and the ₹133cr the June quarter annualises to), ~35x FY2026 actual earnings, and roughly where Paramount Communications (33x) and Finolex Cables (26x) trade — the right neighbourhood for a sub-1%-share cable maker with 84% top-ten customer concentration, three years of negative cash conversion and no institutional price history. Alternatively, ₹272 becomes defensible without a price cut only if two conditions are met after listing: the September-2026 and December-2026 quarters each hold revenue above ₹4,400mn with EBITDA margin above 10%, AND FY2027 operating cash flow turns positive with factored receivables added back. Both are observable within two quarterly filings, and waiting for them costs nothing but a 12.5% grey-market premium.

Why interested

  • A real, defensible niche: ~22.9% of India's ₹3,590cr networking-cable market per 1Lattice, up from ~16% in FY2022, built on a certification stack (ETL, UL, CPR, CE, TSEC, BIS 14493, and the claimed first Indian BIS certification for symmetrical pair/quad cable) that takes customers roughly six months and a plant audit to clear.
  • Demonstrably sticky demand: 93.83% of Q1 FY2027 revenue from retained top-50 customers, top-10 relationships averaging over nine years, customers of ten-plus years contributing 53.63% of FY2026 revenue, no major customer loss in four reporting periods, and bad debts of essentially nil.
  • Backward integration that has visibly worked: in-house copper drawing, 500 MT/month PVC compounding, FRP rods and fibreglass yarn — imported raw material fell from 37.90% of purchases in FY2024 to 6.35% in Q1 FY2027, and top-three supplier reliance from 60.69% to 36.70%.
  • Capacity already in the ground: 895,776 km annualised installed at 30-June-2026 running at ~70%, after ₹1,530mn of capex over FY2025-FY2026 including ₹684.51mn on Unit II, plus a Bengaluru unit live since May 2026 and a commercialised e-beam line — roughly 30% of volume growth available before new money is needed.
  • Deleveraging is accretive arithmetic, not a story: ₹1,555mn repaid out of ₹2,380.96mn of fund-based debt at 7.40-9.00% removes about ₹90-95mn of after-tax interest, roughly 17% of FY2026 profit, before any operating growth.
  • Alignment better than the average promoter-heavy Indian IPO: OFS cut from ₹380cr in the DRHP to ₹232cr with the fresh issue untouched, 58% of the offer primary, promoters retaining 82.17%, no shares pledged, no dividend ever extracted, and a monitoring agency appointed with general corporate purposes inside its remit.
  • The up-mix into specialty power and optical fibre shows up in the physicals, not just the revenue line — realisation rose from ₹21,099 per km in FY2026 to ₹30,749 in Q1 FY2027 as that segment went from 21.37% to 31.23% of sales.
  • End-market demand corroborated outside the commissioned report: fixed-broadband penetration under 5% against 39-49% in developed markets, subscribers up from 18.4mn to 46.5mn since FY2019, and data-centre power capacity up from 0.3 GW to 1.7 GW.
  • Related-party dealing is genuinely immaterial — nothing above 1.05% of revenue in any period, promoter loans fully repaid, a dormant group company under a signed non-compete — which removes an entire category of Indian small-cap risk.

Why cautious

  • The valuation rests on a single quarter with no comparative: Q1 FY2027 revenue equals 41.7% of all of FY2026 and PAT 61% of it, and the auditor's emphasis of matter confirms no June-2025 comparative appears anywhere in the RHP — while the RHP separately states the business is not seasonal.
  • On the audited full year the price is 51.6x FY2026 EPS, 57.5x on post-issue shares and 62.8x after stripping ₹28.72mn of creditor write-backs and ₹32.31mn of FX gain — versus Polycab at 46.7x with a 13.87% margin and net cash, and Finolex at 26.3x debt-free.
  • Profit does not become cash: operating cash flow of −₹97.85mn, −₹266.44mn and −₹117.64mn in the last three periods, cumulatively −₹481.93mn against ₹1,396.65mn of PAT, with free cash flow negative in all four periods on the record.
  • One unnamed customer is 38.54% of Q1 FY2027 revenue; the top ten are 84% of revenue and owe 79.74% of receivables; there are no long-term contracts, only purchase orders, some terminable without cause.
  • Both the working-capital cycle and the cash flow are flattered by structures that appeared only in FY2026: ₹727.36mn of receivables derecognised under limited-recourse factoring at 30-June-2026 (nil before FY2026), and ₹549.73mn of trade payables refinanced into vendor-finance borrowings.
  • FY2026 was deterioration wearing a growth headline — PAT +0.45% on revenue +42%, EBITDA margin 10.17% to 8.23%, RoNW 37.26% to 25.84%, RoCE 41.13% to 23.82%, net debt/EBITDA 0.49x to 2.31x in twenty-four months.
  • Control and compliance history: CARO clause (ii)(b) in all three years with a ₹348.45mn gap between the books and lender statements at March 2026, an MCA s.206(1) notice dated 16-September-2026, delayed statutory dues and CSR transfers, untraceable RoC challans, an environmental non-compliance at Unit II, a CFO change in November 2025 and an auditor change in 2024.
  • The only external credit opinion ever issued on this company is CRISIL B/Stable (Issuer not cooperating), dated November 2024, with none availed since FY2025 — on a balance sheet carrying ₹2,584.57mn of debt against ₹2,687.59mn of equity, entirely on demand facilities.
  • No institutional investor has ever validated a price here. No private round since 2012, promoters' certified cost of acquisition of ₹0.00-₹0.01 per share, and the only internal mark is the FY2026 ESOP fair value of ₹212.96 — 21.7% below the cap.
  • The brand is the company's name and the company does not own it: 'ORIENT' applied for but not granted, three logos unregistered, the logo application opposed by two parties, and Orient Electric pursuing corporate-name rectification under s.16 plus ₹20mn in damages.
  • Half the capex object is hard to justify — 'Pcs' capacity going from 5.04mn to 17.28mn units while that line runs at 6.18% utilisation and contributes 0.39% of revenue — and none of the Objects has been appraised by a bank or financial institution.
  • Working-capital intensity of ~20% of revenue means roughly ₹1,500mn of incremental funding is needed to support FY2027 growth at the June run rate — more than the entire capex object, and the Objects make no provision for it.
  • Sector timing is against the buyer: the RHP's own peer-set average P/E is 128.11x, Sterlite Technologies is up ~560% in twelve months, and the Aditya Birla group entered Indian wires and cables with ₹1,800cr three weeks before this book opened, taking 8-9% off the listed complex in a session.
  • 97% of June-quarter output came from two plants on one leased industrial estate at Bhiwadi, and 88% of an already-small export book (7% of revenue, growing at just 12% CAGR) goes to UAE and Qatar.
  • The deal has been re-timed once: DRHP July 2025 at ₹700cr, exchange approvals September 2025, RHP September 2026 at ₹552cr — fourteen months in which the only thing that materially changed in the company's favour was the June-2026 quarter.

The thesis is wrong if

  • Q2 FY2027 (quarter to September 2026) revenue comes in below ~₹4,400mn, or H1 FY2027 revenue below ~₹9,000mn — i.e. the June quarter was an order pull-forward rather than a run rate. This is the single most important number and it arrives within weeks of listing.
  • FY2027 operating cash flow is again negative, or CFO/PAT stays below 0.5x, despite the ₹1,555mn debt repayment — a fourth consecutive year of profit failing to convert to cash.
  • Trade receivables plus receivables derecognised under the ICICI factoring facility exceed 75 days of trailing revenue at 31 March 2027, or the factored balance grows from the ₹727.36mn at 30 June 2026 — i.e. the reported receivable-day improvement continues to be manufactured.
  • The largest customer remains above 35% of revenue, or the top ten above 80%, in the FY2027 annual report — concentration was supposed to fall as specialty power and new products scaled.
  • EBITDA margin falls back below 9% in any two consecutive quarters, confirming that the 11.22% June-quarter margin was operating leverage on a spike rather than durable mix improvement.
  • Net debt returns above 1.5x EBITDA within eight quarters of listing, or short-term working-capital borrowings exceed the pre-IPO ₹1,942.35mn — i.e. the IPO repaired the balance sheet only temporarily.
  • Inventory stays above ₹1,600mn while quarterly revenue falls, or inventory days exceed 40 at any quarter-end — the ₹567.26mn single-quarter build was not matched by sales.
  • Utilisation of the expanded allied-products ('Pcs') capacity is still below 20% two years after commissioning, or allied products remain under 2% of revenue — confirming the capex object was misdirected.
  • The Orient Electric matter ends in an order or settlement requiring a change to the corporate name or a restriction on use of 'ORIENT' for wires and cables — watch the 13 November 2026 Regional Director and 2 December 2026 Delhi High Court dates.
  • A CARO clause (ii)(b) observation, or any new qualification, appears in the first post-listing annual report — the control remediation management describes did not take.
  • The ₹915mn capex slips materially beyond the disclosed FY2027-FY2029 schedule, or CARE Ratings as monitoring agency reports a deviation in deployment — particularly the general-corporate-purposes tranche being absorbed into working capital.
  • Gross fixed asset turnover, which fell from 8.44x (FY2024) to 5.81x (FY2026), fails to recover above 6.5x by FY2028 once Unit II, Unit III and the e-beam line are fully loaded.

3Business summary

Bhiwadi-based B2B maker of LAN/networking cables and, increasingly, specialty power and optical-fibre cables, selling to a handful of telecom operators, equipment OEMs and resellers.

Orient Cables (India) makes copper data cables — CAT5/5e/6/6A LAN cable, patch cords, CCTV and coaxial cable — plus specialty power cables, low-tension power cable, optical fibre cable (up to 288 fibres), and small volumes of allied passive hardware (keystone jacks, power strips, power cords) and new lines (e-beam irradiated solar cable, EV charging guns/cables, cable harnesses, tethered drone systems). It is purely B2B: customers are telecom service providers (the RHP says it supplies two of India's top three telecom companies), multinational networking-equipment OEMs, IT/system integrators, data-centre builders and cable resellers who rebrand its product. Money is made on manufacturing spread — copper, PVC/HDPE/LDPE compound and masterbatch are bought (copper priced off the LME), converted on 50+ extrusion lines across two Bhiwadi (Rajasthan) plants and a new Bengaluru unit, and sold on purchase orders at 60–90 day credit. The company has integrated backwards into copper processing, PVC compounding (500 MT/month) and FRP rod/fibreglass yarn to protect margin, and it is trying to move up-mix from commodity LAN cable into specialty power/OFC and certified niches (ETL, UL, CPR, CE, BIS 14493). The whole model is working-capital hungry: revenue grew 78% over two years and net working capital went from 9.1% to 20.1% of revenue, funded by short-term bank lines, vendor finance and receivable factoring rather than by operating cash.

10Market share, positioning and moats

The RHP's commissioned 1Lattice report sizes India's wires & cables market at roughly ₹1.17-1.20 lakh crore in FY2026 (derived from its own statements that fibre-optic cable at ₹105.0bn is ~9% of the market and networking cable at ₹35.9bn is ~3%). The two slices the issuer sells into are the fastest-growing: networking cables ₹20.6bn (FY2022) to ₹35.9bn (FY2026) to a projected ₹84.5bn by FY2031 at ~18.7% CAGR, and fibre-optic cables ₹105.0bn to ₹233.1bn at ~17.3%. The demand drivers are real and independently corroborated: fixed-broadband subscribers grew from 18.4mn (FY2019) to 46.5mn (FY2026) with penetration still under 5% against 39-49% in developed markets and ~47% in China; data-centre power capacity went from ~0.3 GW (FY2019) to ~1.7 GW (FY2026) with 10-12 GW projected by FY2031; BharatNet and the National Broadband Mission are live; the RHP also cites an Indian solar e-beam market that grew from ~₹3.2bn (FY2019) to ~₹53.6bn (FY2026). CROSS-CHECK AGAINST INDEPENDENT SOURCES: IMARC sizes India's structured cabling market at USD 841.76mn in 2025 (~₹7,400cr) growing at 8.57% CAGR to 2034; PS Market Research forecasts 12.8% CAGR to 2030 (USD 1,505.2mn from USD 739.9mn in 2024); Grand View forecasts 11% to 2033. These are broader definitions than 1Lattice's cable-only ₹35.9bn, but the growth rates they carry — 8.6% to 12.8% — sit materially below the 18.7% the offer price leans on, and an investor should discount the terminal market accordingly. Near-term sector context is a mania rather than a cycle: Sterlite Technologies is up ~560% in twelve months to ₹853.8 (₹43,925cr market cap, 10-Sep-2026) on FY2026 PAT of ₹56cr, the RHP's own peer-set average P/E is 128.11x, and the Aditya Birla group entered wires & cables with a ₹1,800cr 'Ultravolt' launch in the first week of September 2026 that took 8-9% off the listed cable complex in a session.

Positioning

A credible specialist, not a leader in any market an investor would recognise. Orient Cables occupies the copper data-cable niche — the part of the cable industry the large power-cable companies treat as a side business and the MNC brands mostly import or outsource. Its genuine differentiators are the certification stack (ETL, UL, CPR, CE, TSEC, BIS 14493, and per 1Lattice the first BIS certification in India for symmetrical pair/quad cables), an unusually broad SKU range for its size, and backward integration into copper drawing, PVC compounding (500 MT/month) and FRP/IGFR yarn. It is widening in two directions: up-mix into specialty power and optical fibre (21.4% of FY2026 revenue, 31.2% of Q1 FY2027) and outwards into adjacent hardware (keystone jacks, power cords, EV charging guns, e-beam solar cable, tethered drones). The first is working; the second is, so far, rounding error — ₹47.5mn of combined revenue in FY2026. The strategic weakness is on the customer side: it sells customised product to a very small number of very large buyers, one of whom took 38.54% of Q1 FY2027 revenue, which means pricing power flows the other way. That shows up precisely where you would expect it — an 8.23% FY2026 EBITDA margin against Polycab's 13.87% and KEI's 11.81%.
